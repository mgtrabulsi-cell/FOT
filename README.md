# GameWire

A responsive NFL hub with live scoreboards, weekly matchups, player statistics, and a favorite-centered feed called Your Locker Room.

Site interface copy uses provider-neutral data labels (including "Loading data...") rather than naming the feed provider. Feed URLs, source links, caching, and refresh behavior are unchanged.

Scores refresh immediately when returning to a visible app/tab, restoring a page, or reconnecting; open game box scores and Locker Room plays also refresh on return rather than waiting for their polling timers. Web and native score/box-score requests explicitly asking for fresh data wait for the network read instead of returning a saved-first response. Saved data remains available on connection failures. Regular scoreboard polling remains every 30 seconds, with live box scores and favorite plays every 15 seconds. Closed or OS-suspended apps cannot guarantee background polling.

Fresh score/box reads are not subject to the service worker's four-second saved-response shortcut: they get its full fifteen-second network window (individual scoreboard callers still have a ten-second timeout). Web/native callers can cancel or time out promptly without waiting for a shared offline read. Live web responses return before CacheStorage writes finish, and explicit fresh reads do not trigger a redundant saved-data refresh notification. Capacitor app activation also triggers foreground refresh even if the webview does not emit a visibility event. On mobile (760px or narrower), unrelated NBA box/roster/nearby-date preloads wait until NBA is selected, and full-week offline stat/image downloads pause while games are live; opened content still saves normally, and background weekly saving resumes after the live window. Existing saved browsing is preserved.

NBA pregame comparisons, team season statistics, rosters, Stat Hub directories/scoring order, league browsing, and opened player profiles/game logs also save locally. Saved views render immediately and remain visible offline or after a failed refresh. Stat Hub composes individual saved team rosters even without a saved combined matchup, shows whichever team is ready first, and notices background saves without waiting for its own request. Scoring averages appear progressively without blocking player browsing. Matchup preloading starts from the saved schedule immediately, before the scoreboard refresh finishes. Fresh rosters, team/player season data, and scoring averages are reused for five minutes; full player profiles are reused for one minute. Concurrent requests for the same NBA endpoint share one network read with independent caller cancellation and a ten-second request timeout. Live summary polling still requests fresh data. First-time uncached data requires a network connection, and missing pregame box-score values are never invented.

NBA Stat Hub keeps a protected last-viewed player list, including its displayed player order, separately from the normal rotating data cache. Opening Stat Hub restores that list synchronously even before the selected date's schedule or league/team directory responds, including after the calendar day changes. A note identifies the saved list until current rosters are available; background updates replace it without blocking browsing. Empty/failed roster refreshes do not erase it. Existing saved matchup directories can seed this list after upgrading. Clearing site data or account deletion can remove locally saved lists; the first visit without any saved players still needs a connection.

LIVEWIRE and The Angle restore up to 20 saved published posts synchronously, then refresh without clearing the displayed feed. Their startup snapshots (including Fantasy News) are protected from routine sports-cache eviction. LIVEWIRE notices background preloads even after mounting; Angle feeds preload while their tabs are closed, and saved Angle posts can display before the selected NFL week finishes loading, with a saved-week label. Angle drafts are never stored in public feed snapshots. First use without saved posts requires a connection; refresh errors retain saved posts and offer a retry.

Feed performance: posts already live in the Supabase database; the device snapshots are an additional startup layer, not a second publishing destination. LIVEWIRE subscribes only to its own saved-post updates and skips parent-only renders when its props are unchanged. Identical snapshot saves renew freshness without broadcasting redundant updates or sweeping the whole storage cache. NFL box and previous-night NBA schedule listeners are scoped to their exact resource too. While browsing LIVEWIRE or Analyst Desk, heavy NBA roster/box/nearby-date warming and full-week offline downloads pause so they do not compete with posts/media; normal score polling continues, and sports preloads resume on returning to a sports view. Feed photos remain lazy-loaded; videos wait for playback instead of preloading metadata.

Feed JPG/PNG/WebP photos use public Supabase 640-pixel, quality-75 thumbnails; tapping a photo still opens its unchanged full-size original. GIF animations and videos retain their original URLs. A failed thumbnail is reported and retried using the original. Web and native offline image policies include the same public thumbnail paths (native changes require a rebuilt app); private storage remains excluded.

Our Results restores the complete last-saved published history before fetching, not just the latest feed page. Background refreshes replace the saved history only after all pages succeed, including deletions/unpublishing; failed refreshes retain the prior results with a warning and retry. Successful admin grades are saved locally too. Snapshots omit unused narrative/source/media content, retain every pick and accounting field, and have a protected 1-million-character storage limit; storage errors are logged without truncating records. Refreshes are abortable with a 15-second timeout; periodic refreshes pause in hidden tabs and resume on foreground/reconnect. First use without a saved history still needs a connection, and local saves can be lost when site data is cleared.

Startup no longer imposes an extra one-second splash or replays it after returning from another app. Existing guest sessions can render public views during session restoration; account-only views remain gated. Non-guest session restoration still waits for authentication, and notification onboarding still waits for an authenticated session.

The Angle starts with a collapsed **Active bets** dropdown listing every pending published admin pick across all sports and weeks, independent of the feed's week and player/team filters. It reuses the original pick formatting (including multiline parlays and sprinkle labels) inside separately bordered, numbered cards with plain-text matchup/week context and no links. It excludes settled picks, private drafts, and review attachments. The count is per pick, not per parlay leg. A dedicated saved snapshot makes reopening immediate; background polling every 30 seconds, tab return, foreground/reconnect, and feed edits refresh settlements without clearing the list. A refresh warning identifies potentially stale saved picks when the network fails.

The Angle's published feed is a compact, single-column list of subject lines (post titles). Collapsed titles begin with the first selected theme (Betting, Fantasy, or News), matching the notification prefix; this prefix disappears while expanded. Click or tap a title to expand or collapse its complete post; multiple posts can remain open, and refreshes preserve open posts. Linked stories open automatically. Collapsed posts do not load their galleries or count as read/viewed until expanded into view. Fantasy News, LIVEWIRE, Active bets, and Our Results retain their existing layouts.

NBA pregame list cards on The Wire and matchup details show the reported spread with the home-team abbreviation and its signed line, matching the NFL spread badge. Missing lines are omitted, and spreads are hidden after tipoff. NFL live player boxes warm in the background two games at a time, refreshing stale copies every 30 seconds without downloading full-week player histories or changing scoreboard polling. Open NFL boxes restore saved statistics synchronously, notice background snapshot writes, and continue their existing 15-second live refresh. Failed or wholly empty player refreshes retain the last good box. A first-ever unsaved box still requires a connection.

Wire team favorite markers match both the team ID and league/feed, so favoriting an NBA team cannot mark an unrelated NFL team that shares its provider ID. Matchup favorite controls use the same league-specific matching.

NBA live matchup headers compare reported quarter/overtime and remaining clock against the Wire scoreboard, so an older saved or delayed box response cannot hide a more advanced scoreboard score. Saved player statistics still appear immediately while the summary refreshes; a more advanced summary can update the header without waiting for the next scoreboard poll, including reported score corrections. Live box preloads run ahead of completed games, and open summary subscribers listen only to their own game's saved updates. Scoreboard polling and the existing 15-second open-game refresh are unchanged. A never-saved box still requires its first network read.

NBA player box-score tables retain their team heading and all stat columns even before any player data has been downloaded. Saved rows stay visible throughout refreshes and errors. Incomplete team/player/stat responses cannot erase a good saved or currently displayed box, even if local storage is unavailable or cleared; an explicit refresh warning remains until a complete update succeeds. Unknown statistics are never invented. Stat Hub always renders both selected matchup teams in away/home order, even with only one cached roster. Each available roster appears without waiting for the other; loading/failure messages remain inside the missing team's panel. Empty roster refreshes retain prior nonempty rosters. Last-viewed lists still restore before a schedule arrives, but a selected matchup never substitutes an unrelated game's teams.

NBA teams on The Wire list and matchup headers display a **B2B** badge when a completed game is reported for that team on the previous Eastern calendar date. Each badge opens that exact game's box score inside GameWire. Previous-night schedules load first in nearby-date preloading, reuse saved schedules immediately, and update badges when background saves arrive. Missing or unplayed games never produce a badge.

Published attached settled picks in The Angle and LIVEWIRE show only the pick text and its green check or red X, without review headings, explanations, or original-post metadata. Admin selection guidance stays in the editor; attached picks still do not count a second time in Our Results.

### Shared sports database

GameWire also keeps public NFL/NBA schedules, team directories, and rosters in Supabase's `sports_resources` table. New devices request the shared schedule alongside the normal live-score request; a late saved response cannot replace fresh results. Schedule failures can fall back to the shared copy, labeled as saved because scores/status may be stale. Rosters and NBA team directories use the shared database first, with direct-feed fallback for missing resources or database failures. Device snapshots and web/native offline caches remain enabled; live summaries and normal scoreboard polling stay direct and unchanged.

The secret-protected `sync-sports-resources` function runs hourly through the existing Vault/pg_cron setup, without requiring an open browser. It saves NFL's current scoreboard and all 18 regular-season weeks, plus NBA schedules from seven days ago through 30 days ahead. Schedules refresh hourly (NFL future-week resources reuse six-hour copies); directories and all 32 NFL/30 NBA rosters refresh every 12 hours. Failed, malformed, or unexpectedly empty refreshes do not overwrite good resources. Empty schedules for dates without games are supported. Data is public-read-only; only the server service role can write it. This improves feed-outage resilience, not guaranteed availability during database/hosting outages.

Backend setup: apply `supabase/sports-resources.sql` to the linked project, deploy `sync-sports-resources` with `--no-verify-jwt` (it validates the existing cron secret itself), and run the scheduled job once to seed it before releasing the client. Existing `gamewire-poll-url` and `gamewire-poll-secret` Vault entries and `GAMEWIRE_CRON_SECRET` function configuration are required. Verify row coverage and `cron.job` after deployment. `supabase/tests/sports-resources.sql` checks anonymous reads and denial of anonymous/authenticated writes in a rolled-back transaction.

## GamewAIre setup

### GamewAIre Player Lab

All signed-in accounts enter GamewAIre on the **GamewAIre Bot** tab, with Player Lab as the main feature. The second tab, **GamewAIre Best Bets and Leans**, contains the Analysis Engine banner, picks, records, results and existing permission-protected admin tools. Arrow keys, Home and End switch tabs; switching preserves the current player analysis and best-bet filters without remounting either panel.

The navy/neon-blue welcome banner uses the site's GamewAIre typography, a glowing bot icon and heartbeat accents. Search current NFL QB/RB/WR/TE rosters by name or team, select PPR, half-PPR or standard, and open one weekly player analysis. This is a **search-only, rules-based analysis tool**, not a conversational chatbot: there is no message composer, topic-reply UI or conversation history, and no AI provider key is needed. Closing and reopening preserves the selected analysis in the current tab. Desktop (1100px and wider) retains the GamewAIre page's 90% scale, with the lab at an additional 94%; smaller headings, tighter spacing and compact phone cards show more information without shrinking 16px phone inputs or 44px touch targets. Guests and anonymous sessions cannot call the endpoint. Member access to the lab does not grant admin analysis, private metrics, publishing or notifications.

The player-search bar occupies its own full-width row below the welcome graphic's feature strip on all screen sizes.

Selecting another player replaces the previous analysis, including while the new outlook loads. The search field's **Clear player search** control clears typed text without removing an outlook. The **Selected players** area provides **Clear player** beside each selected name, removing that player's outlook and returning focus to search. **Compare** keeps two distinct player slots: choose a slot, then search to fill or replace it. Clearing one comparison player preserves the other. Player clearing is disabled while data loads. Changing scoring refreshes both, with explicit per-player errors and retry support. **Exit Compare** returns to the first player's outlook.

On phones (600px and below), Compare uses blue/red portrait cards with a VS badge, followed by **Overview, Stats, Props, Matchup and Sources** tabs. Overview retains weekly verdicts, availability, snapshots and full quick-take reasoning. Stats aligns the union of both players' actual recorded metrics in one accessible table, with each player's own game count and historical fantasy scoring (not a projection). Props aligns existing estimates and retains expandable full estimate context and published props; missing metrics remain unavailable rather than zero. Matchup and Sources retain all existing report content. Bars compare numeric magnitude, not player quality or a recommended choice. Tabs support arrow keys/Home/End and never request new reports. Larger screens retain the two full report columns. Clear, replacement, scoring, retry and refresh behavior are unchanged.

Deploy `supabase/gamewire-bot.sql` after GamewAIre access setup, then deploy `gamewire-bot` with `--no-verify-jwt` (the handler verifies the user JWT with `getUser` itself). It needs only the standard Supabase URL/anon/service-role environment variables. Cache/usage tables and the atomic refresh-claim RPC remain service-role-only. Cache misses use an independent **60-refresh-per-account-per-hour** safety limit; cached responses remain usable. The claim function accepts existing non-anonymous, non-deleted, non-banned accounts; it does not depend on owner/admin roles. No direct member grants to caches, rankings or usage tables are added. `supabase/tests/gamewire-bot.sql` verifies owner and member allowances and denied direct access in a rolled-back transaction. Apply this SQL before deploying the member endpoint and frontend.

ESPN supplies the current season/week, current team roster, regular-season player game logs and verified opponent scoring-defense rank. Search caches for fifteen minutes, workload for five minutes and current-week scoreboard for one minute; visible checked-at timestamps identify snapshots. Failed roster subsets and unavailable matchup rankings are explicitly reported. A blocked roster/game-log request yields an explicit HTTP 206 partial response and visible source errors, cached for only thirty seconds: available published editorial guidance remains readable, but unavailable workload/scoring stays empty, and unavailable roster status prevents a Start verdict. Provider failures never fall back to demonstration profile data or invented stats. No paid odds or AI requests are made. Props come **only** from already-published GamewAIre picks with an exact ESPN player and event match; old/different-week picks and prices worse than -200 are excluded. Saved lines show their timestamp/book and are not represented as current prices. This bot does not analyze games, publish picks or send notifications.

The result layout includes a player portrait, Start/Sit/source cards, **Prop Lab**, and a branded **GamewAIre Verdict**, inspired by the supplied concept without made-up AI confidence scores. Each request also reads **only published** Start/Sit graphic content for the exact NFL season/week and matching player/team/position, including the author's reasoning. Unpublished drafts are never used. The bot combines that guidance, any matching weekly ranking, and recent workload; availability and game locks take precedence. Graphics do not specify a scoring format, which is disclosed. Conflicting Start/Sit publications yield Monitor rather than a silently chosen verdict.

Scoreboard, team-directory, roster and position-game source failures try ESPN's equivalent public API host and disclose that source change without repeating a notice for every team. If neither public feed can verify the current week, the bot reports an error rather than reusing an unverified week or fabricating current matchups.

Admins can open **Weekly top-30 player rankings** below Player Lab in the **GamewAIre Bot** tab. Select a week, scoring format and QB/RB/WR/TE, then paste up to 30 names (one per line, optional numbered prefixes/team abbreviations), review exact roster matches, or use player pickers. Ambiguous/unmatched names require manual selection. Reorder with Up/Down; remove or add players; publish between 1 and 30 distinct players. Publishing atomically replaces only that season/week/format/position list. Errors preserve the editor draft; changing scope warns before discarding unpublished changes. Lists are not prepopulated, and missing players are not automatically Sit. Both endpoint actions require the existing admin permission; the table and replacement RPC remain service-role-only. Lists live in `gamewire_fantasy_rankings` with verified ESPN player/team IDs and publication time. QB/TE top 12 and RB/WR top 24 provide typical 12-team context alongside workload. A matching published ranking also adds a linear editorial multiplier from +5% at rank 1 to -5% at rank 30 to available estimates, never inventing missing metrics or overriding availability/game locks. Lists are reread after the workload cache, so refreshing a player uses newly published rankings without waiting for cache expiry. Each scoring format needs its own list.

Without published guidance, the transparent workload guide uses the latest three recorded current-season appearances, not a trained fantasy model: **Start** requires QB 25+ pass attempts, RB 15+ touches, WR 7+ targets (8 standard) or TE 5+ targets (6 standard). **Flex** requires RB 8+ touches, WR 4+ targets or TE 3+ targets; lower usage indicates **Sit**. Published weekly graphics explicitly produce **Must Start / Must Sit**, not "consideration" labels. A matching top-12 QB/TE or top-24 RB/WR ranking plus Start-level workload also supports Must Start; a top-30 rank alone does not. Missing workload, fewer than three appearances, a 21-day data gap or uncertain roster availability yields Monitor unless verified published guidance is available; availability and game locks always take precedence. Preseason/postseason indications are unavailable. These rules do not confirm a starting role, account for lineup alternatives, or replace official game-day injury reports. Complete historical fantasy scoring is displayed only when all required fields are present; missing stats are never zeros. Scoring excludes bonuses and two-point conversions.

**Prop Lab yardage and reception estimates** are separate from sportsbook lines and the existing GamewAIre pick model. Verified current-season logs are merged by event with up to five recent completed team box scores, recovering estimates when logs are missing or incomplete without double-counting games. The baseline uses 65% recent-three / 35% available season production. At least one recent verified player performance, a measured position-group share and one opponent position-game sample are required. Fewer than three samples on either side produces a visible **Limited-data estimate**, a 15% rather than 30% matchup weight, and a tighter +/-15% cap. Otherwise the blend is 70% player baseline / 30% positional allowance times share. Actual ESPN depth-chart order caps backup opportunity; absent or ambiguous depth uses explicitly labeled recent usage (attempts/targets where reported, otherwise recorded production). WR formation slots are treated as concurrent starting slots, not ordered WR1/WR2 roles. Backup share caps are 40% for second-depth RB/TE, 20% for deeper RB/TE, 40%/25% for second/deeper WR depth, and 15% for backup QBs; measured share already below a cap is not penalized again. Position-group totals are never presented as one player's expected production. QB passing/rushing, RB rushing/receiving and WR/TE receiving yards are supported, plus **RB/WR/TE receptions**. Catches use actual counts, not yards or lines; yards round to whole numbers and catches to one decimal. Missing, negative or fractional catch counts cannot supply a sample; unclassified nonzero production excludes that position sample. Report cache v6 separates the new methodology from previous estimates.

Up to three seasons of logs supply direct opponent history; two verified meetings add 10%. When both current teams are division top-two, three individual games against verified winning contenders can add 5%: both clubs must have had a .600 record and four prior games **before that historical kickoff**. Limited-data estimates omit these historical components. Stakes alone never create a numeric boost. Normal matchup adjustments cap at +/-25%; **verified bottom-four WR/TE yardage defenses** add 8% (3% with limited player samples), with a +35% ceiling for full samples. These ranks compare up to five recent completed same-position receiving-yardage games for all 32 defenses, requiring at least three verified samples per team; ties are not arbitrarily broken. WR and TE are separate; team scoring rank and team names, including Dallas, cannot trigger this boost. League positional coverage caches for fifteen minutes; failures are disclosed and omit the extra boost while retaining supported player estimates. Ranking editorial adjustments apply afterward, bounded separately at +/-5%. These are experimental heuristic weights, **not fitted, backtested or calibrated predictions**, independent of sportsbook lines/prices. Cards retain concise blurbs plus role, positional-rank and limited-sample labels, without model-input tables. Final boxes cache 24 hours; other sources fifteen minutes. Requests remain on ESPN; no paid odds calls, pick publications or notifications are added. Existing Top Picks/game leans are unchanged.

Enabling actual conversation later requires a separately configured server-side AI provider, grounded-response validation and explicit usage/cost controls. Conversation remains disabled for every account in this search-only release.

## Offline access

After opening the production site online once, GameWire saves its app shell for offline launches, including from a phone's Home Screen. Once the offline worker controls the page, it proactively saves the displayed NFL week's game summaries, both teams' player/season stats, recent-game sources, team logos, and player faces, even for games and team tabs you have not opened. It repeats every five minutes while open, visible, and online, and retries on reconnect or when returning to the app after that interval. Saving runs quietly without an online save-status banner on the website or native apps. Existing live-score polling remains unchanged. Browsing The Wire, Stat Hub, and LIVEWIRE also saves their public responses. Offline browsing is read-only: private account data, favorites sync, sign-in, publishing, and new notifications require internet.

When disconnected, an offline banner warns that scores, game statuses, stats, odds, and projections are snapshots, not live updates. It shows the oldest saved response used on the current page when available and any offline-save errors. Other weeks, searches, filters, matchup-board projections, and older post pages still need to be opened online before going offline. Weekly player faces/logos are explicitly downloaded rather than relying on the browser's temporary image cache. Previously viewed public LIVEWIRE photos are also saved. Upstream missing stats or photos cannot be invented; unavailable requests, storage failures, or incomplete saves are logged. The Locker Room's private account history is not cached by this feature.

The website also writes successful public ESPN/post responses directly into device-local Cache Storage before returning them to the UI, without waiting for service-worker activation. Logos, faces, and public post photos are stored as readable image bytes and rendered from local blob URLs, avoiding a competing initial remote image request. These copies share the worker's bounded public/image stores; cold offline launches can reuse them, and failed refreshes never overwrite the last successful data. A failed scoreboard poll also retains the currently displayed games. On reload, The Wire immediately shows the last saved board for the selected week (including while sign-in restores), and scoreboard requests time out after 10 seconds. With nothing saved, it shows loading placeholders, then a "Retry now" message with automatic 5-second retries, instead of "No NFL games". Weekly warming saves team logos first and player images per matchup rather than waiting for the entire week's stats to finish. A missing image does not stop later image batches, and a failed team save does not skip its opponent; incomplete saves are still recorded as failures. The app requests persistent browser storage when supported, but browser eviction, clearing site data, private browsing, and content never downloaded cannot be covered. Live data is saved on each device, not committed into the source code; reconnecting resumes fresh ESPN reads.

Public API reads display a successful saved response first, without waiting for ESPN, then revalidate quietly online. Concurrent refreshes of the same query/format/filter share one request, with a 15-second minimum between checks to avoid update-triggered loops. Changed response bodies are saved before a batched notification refreshes the affected scoreboard, player/stat panels, news, LIVEWIRE feed, or matchup board. Unchanged responses do not trigger UI updates; existing stats stay visible during refresh and failed refreshes retain the last good copy. Native foreground reads likewise use a disk-only `peek` before background revalidation through the shared native store. Content not previously saved still needs its first download; reading local files and rendering are not literally instantaneous. Only ESPN feeds and the configured project's public LIVEWIRE/matchup-board reads are eligible. Authentication, role checks, private tables, RPCs, writes, and error responses are never cached. Theme/player filters and response formats keep separate cache keys. Public response storage is bounded to 1,500 requests (at most 5 MB each), with 2,500 runtime images to accommodate a full NFL week including defensive players rather than evicting faces after a few teams. Older entries are evicted. Image warming uses four concurrent downloads and game warming two concurrent games. New builds replace the shell cache without touching existing notification routing. Cached public responses may remain after sign-out because they are public. Phones do not guarantee background timers when the app is closed or suspended; no automatic refresh is promised in that state.

Reload/reopen once online after an update to install the new offline worker. Offline access requires a secure origin (HTTPS or localhost), service-worker support, and browser storage; private browsing, storage pressure, or device cleanup can remove saved content. Run `node --test scripts/offline.test.mjs scripts/notification-routing.test.mjs` to check cache rules, isolation, and notification behavior. To verify in a browser, use a production preview (`npm run build`, then `npm run preview`), load public views online, enable network offline mode, and reload. Vite development mode does not cache the development module graph.

For immediate repeat openings, successful loads also retain assembled public scoreboard, player-table, game-box-score, team-average, and roster-detail snapshots in local storage. The Wire and Stat Hub read these synchronously during rendering instead of rebuilding them through the full schedule/roster/history/player-request chain. They are keyed by feed, season, team/game, and selected week; saved totals stay visible if a refresh fails. Full-week warming starts after a five-second opening grace period to reduce startup contention. This additional display store is bounded to 160 entries, roughly 4 MB of UTF-16 text, and 256,000 characters per entry. Rosters are saved once per team/game, not duplicated for both single-team and combined views, and player-table snapshots retain only displayed players. It contains no authentication or private account data and is cleared on account deletion with late writes disabled. Existing installations need one successful load with this version to create assembled display snapshots; never-downloaded data still requires ESPN.

### Native iPhone and Android apps

The Capacitor projects in [`ios/`](ios/) and [`android/`](android/) bundle the React interface rather than loading the website remotely. Native tooling requires Node.js 22 or newer. Run `npm run native:sync` to build `dist-native` and copy it to both projects, then `npm run native:ios` (on a Mac with Xcode) or `npm run native:android` (Android Studio/JDK 21/SDK 36). Normal `npm run build` and GitHub Pages deployment remain separate. `us.gamewire.app` is the initial bundle/application identifier; confirm its availability under your developer accounts before signing or publishing.

Native `GameWireOffline` bridges use shared on-device files for successful public ESPN and public LIVEWIRE/matchup-board reads and sports/post images. Foreground reads, five-minute weekly saves, and background jobs access the same files; the image component resolves them through Capacitor's local file server. Cache keys preserve exact queries, Accept, and fantasy filters. Auth, private tables, RPCs, writes, bearer tokens, and cookies are not replayed or saved. Only the public API key is retained for anonymous public-table refresh. Native public caches are excluded from backups and bounded to 4,000 files/512 MB; eviction during a snapshot reports an incomplete save, and atomic writes preserve good copies on failed downloads. Account deletion clears native files and cancels scheduled refresh. Native WebViews do not use the web service worker.

Android WorkManager requests unique 30-minute periodic work, with internet and non-low-battery constraints. iOS registers `BGAppRefreshTask` at launch, declares only the `fetch` background mode, requests an earliest start 30 minutes later, and resubmits after each invocation. No audio, location, exact alarms, or silent-push tricks are used to keep the app alive. Both systems control execution and can defer or skip it; iPhone force-quit, disabled Background App Refresh, battery restrictions, and lack of connectivity can prevent updates. Reopening the native app online starts an immediate foreground snapshot regardless of the five-minute timer.

Background jobs incrementally replay previously saved public requests, prioritizing the current scoreboard then the oldest saved requests, with a ~24-second budget and a maximum of 48 requests per invocation, then download discovered missing ESPN images if time permits. They do not promise to refresh a whole NFL week within Apple's short execution window or fetch unseen weeks automatically. Successful responses replace individual copies; failures retain previous copies. Native refresh status retains last attempt, count, remaining requests/images, and incomplete-copy details for diagnostics without displaying an online banner. Foreground saving fills any gaps. A fresh install needs one successful online visit before there is a background request list.

The manually dispatched [`Validate native apps`](.github/workflows/native-build.yml) workflow provides separate Linux/Android and macOS/iOS compilation without signing or publishing. Compilation alone does not prove OS scheduling: test physical iPhone and Android devices, background and cold-offline launches, disabled refresh, low battery, expired sessions, and deletion while refresh is running. In Xcode, use the registered `us.gamewire.app.public-refresh` task identifier to simulate launch/expiration while debugging; a real scheduling test must also be performed outside the debugger.

This is native background-refresh groundwork, not an App Store approval guarantee or submission-ready release. Before submission, configure signing/developer accounts, replace generated app icons/splash assets, verify data/photo/logo licensing, supply privacy policy and accurate App Privacy/Data Safety disclosures, complete the privacy-manifest review, test login/password-reset deep links, and implement APNs/FCM native push if notifications are part of the native listing (browser Web Push is deliberately disabled in native builds). ### NBA push notifications

NBA alerts use the existing per-device **Game updates** (`notify_scores`) preference. An enabled device receives:

- **Favorite NBA teams only:** cumulative scores at completed quarters, halftime, overtime boundaries and Final. Favoriting a player alone does not enable team quarter alerts. Missing quarter linescores are retried, not replaced with zeros; after missed polls, only the latest completed boundary is reported rather than a backlog.
- **Any NBA player, regardless of favorites:** one alert at each crossed threshold of **35, 40, 50 and 60 points**. A poll that crosses multiple thresholds queues each one; corrections below an already-reported threshold do not trigger it again.
- **35+ points at Final:** one reported **PTS / REB / AST** line per qualifying player, including exactly 35. Missing rebounds/assists delay this alert rather than inventing values.
- **Halftime buzzer-beaters:** reported made two-/three-point field goals at exactly 0:00 of Q2 once halftime is confirmed. Free throws, misses, unconfirmed clocks and other quarter-ending shots do not qualify.
- **Game-winning buzzer-beaters:** reported made two-/three-point field goals at exactly 0:00 of the final regulation/overtime period, only after Final confirms the shot turned a tie/deficit into the winning final score. Tying shots, shots that merely extend a lead, and plays inconsistent with the final score are excluded. ESPN must supply enough play/score data to confirm the event; unavailable plays are not guessed.

### Team matchup-history leads

**Analyst Desk → The Angle → Admin desk** also scans ESPN's calendar-year NFL scoreboards for completed regular-season and playoff meetings between each selected matchup's teams. It uses a rolling five-calendar-year window, inclusive of the starting instant and exclusive of kickoff; the selected game and results dated in the future are excluded. A leap-day start clamps to February 28. The minimum win-rate sample is four meetings, and wins divided by all meetings must be **strictly greater than 70%** (ties count in the denominator, not as wins).

Additional leads flag an unbeaten record in the latest four meetings overall, or the latest four at the upcoming game's home/away configuration, all within the same five-year window. Four unbeaten meetings can include ties and are not labeled four wins. Neutral-site finals count overall but never toward home/away leads; no venue-specific claim is made for an upcoming neutral-site game or when venue classification is unknown. These are franchise/team-ID records, not stadium, coach, roster, ATS, or player-performance trends.

Each private lead includes its W-L-T record, sample size, date window, and individual ESPN game links. **Review this lead** carries the team focus, source, and computed supporting context into an unpublished editor; the admin must still write the headline/storyline and verify the claims before publishing. History is fetched only for admins, in batches of three calendar years, and cached in memory for ten minutes. Failed years suppress affected matchup leads rather than calculate a rate from partial history; malformed final scores and conflicting duplicate games are reported. No qualifying trend is a valid result. Run `node --test scripts/angle-history.test.mjs scripts/the-angle.test.mjs` for threshold, venue, date-window, parsing, and failure regressions.

Your Locker Room combines ESPN headlines matched to favorite names with score/status changes and big-play alerts for favorited NFL players. Scoreboards poll every 30 seconds, latest plays every 15 seconds during relevant live games, and news every five minutes. ESPN public feeds have no availability guarantee.
